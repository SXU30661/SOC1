Alert triage is a complex process that often requires analysts to gather additional information about affected employees or servers. htis is basically a method of how effectively and effeciently an analyst is going to follow or as reference in dealing with alerts.

**SOC workbook**, also called playbook, runbook, or workflow, is a structured document that defines the steps required to investigate and remediate specific threats efficiently and consistently. Since L1 analysts are considered junior specialists and are not expected to triage every possible attack scenario perfectly, senior analysts often prepare workbooks to support their less experienced teammates. L1 analysts are recommended and sometimes even required to triage the alerts precisely according to workbooks to avoid mistakes and streamline the analysis.

First looking into **Identity Inventory**. Knowing who and what is involve on the alert. 
This contains any employee's status on the company, role, work hours, user accounts, relations. machine accounts, privileges, contacts etc. It helps SOC analyst to get an overview or context of the involved user and machine. 

Sources of identities. 

Active Directory.  Example On-Prem AD, Entra ID
SSO Providers. OKTA, Google Workspace
HR Systems. BambooHR, SAP, HiBob
Custom Solutions. CSV or Excel Sheets


Second is  **Asset Inventory**

Asset inventory, also called asset lookup, is a list of all computing resources within an organisation's IT environment. Often refers to software, Hardware, employees, machine accounts. workstations, servers etc. 

Sources of Assets

Active Directory.  Example On-Prem AD, Entra ID
SEIM or EDR. Elastic, Crowdstrike
MDM Solution. Ms Intune, Jamf MDM 
Custom Solution.  CSV or Excel Sheets

Continuing identity and asset inventory topics, you might also need to look at the alert from a network point of view, especially in bigger companies

Different teams have different approaches to workbook building. Some teams may have hundreds of complex workbooks for every possible detection rule, more like a automation playbook than human guides. Other teams may prepare just a few high-level workbooks for the most common attack vectors and rely more on the experience and decision-making of L1 analysts. In any case, you as an L1 analyst should know how to divide your investigation into modular blocks and build simple workbooks around it.
